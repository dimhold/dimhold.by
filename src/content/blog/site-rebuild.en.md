This domain sat frozen for seven years. A 2019 Jekyll blog with `title: Your awesome title` still in the config, two nav links leading to commented-out pages, and a finance dashboard hotlinking charts that no longer load. The classic personal site: built in a two-week burst, abandoned by April.

I rebuilt it in a day. Here is what's under the hood, including the parts that went sideways.

## A financial model you can poke

At [Adlega](https://adlega.com) we have one non-negotiable design decision: the AI never does the math. Agents interview you, fill the model, explain any number — but every figure comes from a deterministic engine we test against a hand-built Excel model.

I wanted that idea to be something you can touch, so the Adlega card on the front page has a toy: a 36-month MRR projection with two sliders, growth and churn, and a goal-seek button that solves "what growth rate triples MRR in 24 months". The closed form is one line:

```ts
const net = Math.pow(3, 1 / 24);          // required net monthly factor
const growth = net / (1 - churn) - 1;     // solve (1+g)(1-c) = net
```

Not one number in that widget was generated by a model. That's the point, and the caption says so.

## The avatar that came out of a dead internet

I wanted a 3D me on the page — poke it, drag it, it watches your cursor. The plan was Ready Player Me. Turns out the domain doesn't resolve anymore. Not blocked, not down for maintenance — gone.

Plan B was [Avaturn](https://avaturn.me): selfie in, GLB out. The export advertised "avatar with animation". The animation turned out to be a single frame, 0.033 seconds long, freezing the model mid-dance-move like a man falling off an invisible chair. And without the clip the model stands in a full T-pose, arms out like a scarecrow.

So: bone surgery in three.js. Rotate `LeftArm` and `RightArm` down, bend the forearms a touch. First attempt did nothing visible — I was rotating around Z, which on a Mixamo-style rig runs *along* the bone, so I was just twisting the arms in place. The X axis brings them down. Then the head drooped: I was overwriting the head bone's rest rotation with an absolute look-at value. Capture the rest pose first, apply cursor offsets on top of it.

None of this is hard. All of it is invisible when it works, which is the usual deal with 3D on the web.

## Boring choices, on purpose

Everything else is deliberately unexciting. [Astro 5](https://astro.build), static output, GitHub Pages, zero frameworks on the client — the interactivity is vanilla TypeScript plus three.js, lazy-loaded so the text renders instantly. Three languages — English, Russian, Belarusian — as three static page trees with proper `hreflang`. Analytics without cookies: PostHog with in-memory persistence, so there's no banner because there's nothing to consent to.

The 2019 site died because it was a chore. This one is a toy I want to keep playing with. That's the entire retention strategy.
