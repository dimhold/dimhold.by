I took a list of npm package names, split the 2,448,677 unscoped ones by the hyphen and counted the last word. The top is what you would guess: `cli`, `ui`, `test`, `sdk`, `lib`. In 10th place, between `components` and `utils`, is `breki` with 11,573 names. `sukiwir` is 13th, `sluey` is 14th, `riris` is 15th. I have never seen any of these words in a dependency tree.

I came for something else. The question was how many packages look generated by their metadata alone and did the share go up in the year of coding agents. When I open an unknown package I look at the repository link, the number of versions and the README. From that I decide if a person stands behind it. How often this habit is wrong I never measured.

Spam in npm is well described. In April 2024 Phylum counted 14,000 packages registered with the tea.xyz protocol in all open source registries together, Socket wrote about the same wave and in July about a new one. They were looking for campaigns, I only counted signs in a random sample.

## What I took

The names come from `all-the-package-names` 2.0.2223 of October 3, it is 3,605,033 names including the 1,156,356 scoped ones. From them I took 3,000 at random with a fixed seed and asked the registry for the full document of each. Only versions published up to October 3 count. The document was usable for 2,774 names. For 226 names there was nothing to read (79 of them are npm security placeholders) and they are not in the shares below. I wrote the criteria down before the download.

## The generator is inside the package

First the strange words. 732 last words have at least 300 names each. I computed for each the share of names that look like `wawan-bubur7-wekto`, that is a word, a word with a number, a word. In 717 cases this share is at most 3.3%, in 15 it is at least 86.7%. Between them it is empty.

## My rule

Back to the sample. I had 5 signs: no repository, a single version, a README that is missing, shorter than 200 characters or a starter template, no description, a numbered family of at least 20 names. A package with 3 or more looks generated. I expected 2025 to be several times above the years before ChatGPT.

It did not happen. 2025 is below 2024 and about the same as 2021, which has 24.5% (the intervals overlap, from 17% to 28% for 2025 and from 19% to 30% for 2021). ## What I didn't check

Names with nothing to read are not in my shares and 55 of these 226 belong to the `breki` family. If I put all 55 into 2024, its share is from 30.3% (the rule flags none of them) to 39.6% (it flags all). A package from 2025 had less time to get a second version, this pushes its share up and it is still lower than 2024. The thresholds of 200 characters, 20 names and 3 signs are mine. The README in the rule is the one the registry keeps for the whole package, so for a package updated later it may be newer than the rest. 2 packages with a trace of tea.xyz are too few to say what the `breki` family was made for.
