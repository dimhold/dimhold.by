# Belarusian folk games — what actually exists

Groundwork for the Гульні section. Researched in Belarusian and Russian against
the state heritage databank, the intangible-heritage inventory, a peer-reviewed
article from Polack State University, and the online full texts of Lozka's
*Беларускі фальклор*, BNT *Дзіцячы фальклор* (1972) and *Беларускія народныя
гульні* (1991).

---

## The finding that changes the brief

**The section cannot honestly be built on "games unique to Belarus", because
there are almost none.**

The corpus is distinctive in **vocabulary, song text, implement and calendar
placement**. It is largely **shared in mechanics** with Russia, Ukraine and
Poland. This is not a gap in the research — it is what the Belarusian
ethnographers themselves write. Where they comment comparatively, they
generally *deny* uniqueness:

- Lozka on «А мы проса сеялі»: *«Вядомы ўсім усходнім славянам»* — and he adds
  that the **flax and hemp carahods were formed on the model of «Проса»**. The
  author of the standard reference undercuts the most obvious pick from inside.
- The 1991 Belarusian text of «У каменьчыкі» says in its own words that the
  game was **borrowed from Jewish girls**.
- The BNT commentary on «Цурка» records that Cіiaraščanka **considered it
  Ukrainian**.
- Adam Fischer, *Lud polski* (1926), records «Свінка» as universal among Polish
  village boys, and places «Пікер» on Mazowsze, Podlasie and in Wileńszczyzna.

**Exactly three uniqueness claims exist anywhere in the literature**, plus a set
of regional *localisations* that are not the same thing. They are in Tier 1
below. Everything else has to be presented as *the Belarusian version of* — with
its own name, its own words and its own tune, which is the genuinely interesting
part and a better story than exclusivity.

The alternative framing, and the one this section should use: **not "games only
Belarus has", but "how Belarus played"** — the particular text, the particular
implement, the particular place in the farming year.

## Two bibliographic corrections

**There is no book «А. Ю. Лозка. Беларускія народныя гульні. Мінск, 2003».** The
2003 Minsk volume is **«Гульні, забавы, ігрышчы»**, the games volume of the
academic BNT series — 1st ed. 1996, Беларуская навука, 534 pp., reissued 2000
and 2003, with Lozka credited for the introduction, classification and
commentary. It is the largest Belarusian games collection ever published and it
is **not online**. A separate, much smaller **«Беларускія народныя гульні»
(Мінск: Юнацтва, 1991, 42 pp., 33 games)** *is* online, and is largely re-edited
Ramanaŭ and Šejn material.

**Lozka's teaching manual «Народныя гульні» is permanently lost**, not
temporarily down: `nastaunik.eu` serves an Azure "web app is stopped" page and
the Wayback CDX index holds **zero captures** of the file. His classification
survives in his textbook *Беларускі фальклор*, which is online in full.

### The Яшчар warning

Яшчар is the most beautiful thing in the corpus and the easiest to misrepresent.

It is **East Slavic in origin.** The Ukrainian «Ящур» has a nearly identical
golden-throne opening. Russian analogues run «Яша», «Олей», «Олень», «Уля»,
«Тюлень», «Лень». Moszyński recorded «Олей» in Mazyr district in 1914, and Lozka
himself met «Яшчар» and «Олей» **together** in Žytkavičy district in 1986.

The defensible claim is Lozka's own — *«беларускія варыянты выглядаюць больш
кансерватыўнымі, архаічнымі»* — plus the fact that the Pastavy form is the only
one anywhere under state protection. That is a good, true, interesting claim.
"Unique to Belarus" is not.

One more correction: the incipit **«Сядзіць Яшчар пад дубам» does not appear in
any recorded Belarusian variant.** Every recording opens *«Сядзіць, сядзіць
Яшчар…»* followed by a locative, and the locative is exactly where the variants
split: **«ой на моры»** (Мажэйкі), **«на куце»** (Віцебшчына — the кут is the
sacred corner where the dead were laid out, ethnographically the loaded one),
**«у залатым крэсле»** (also the shared East Slavic form), **«пшанічаньку
точа»** (Лагойск). The refrain «ладу, ладу» is a later accretion and is **not
recorded in Pastavy district at all**, which is part of why those variants are
judged the most archaic.

The structural fact worth designing around: the song has **two halves in
opposition**. Part one is praise-and-movement in a circling rhythm. Part two is
the redemption pleading, and Bierkovič's musicological analysis found its melody
**close to lament-type wedding tunes**.

## Tier 2 — strongly localised, no uniqueness claim, genuinely unusual

No one calls these unique, but no close analogue turned up either, and several
carry folk design ideas worth having.

- **«Панначка»** — girls' carahod, each moving with a **lit candle**, trying to
  blow out a neighbour's while shielding her own; the last flame relights the
  rest. Recorded on Hrodna region. **Visually the strongest thing in the whole
  corpus.**
- **«Грахі»** — a ball is rolled along a row of holes. A miss puts a **грэх**
  (pebble) in your own hole, a hit puts one in the victim's; three sins and you
  are "shot" against a wall. The distinctive rule: **players carrying sins get
  fewer throws — guilt reduces your right to punish others.** Hole-and-ball is
  shared across Europe; that moral bookkeeping is real folk design.
- **«Азярод»** — slam a springy horse-collar bow against the ground so it vaults
  over the **азярод**, the pole rack for drying hay. Depends on a specifically
  Belarusian farm structure. **No analogue found anywhere.**
- **«Гуські»** — on cleared ice, billets at the centre and one nest-hole per
  player at the rim; on the signal everyone charges with forked metre sticks to
  drive гуські into their own hole. No close Russian or Polish twin found.
- **«Браднік»** — a blindfolded drag-net fisherman gropes for "fish" who
  **cooperatively call «Глыбока!» / «Мелка!» to steer him**; a catch must be
  identified by voice, and the catch answers with a fish name. Blind man's buff
  with sonar and a recognition duel.
- **«Мянькі»** — fish shelter in drawn pits, one per pit, so an arriving fish
  evicts the occupant. **The winners are the five who sheltered longest**, not
  the last uncaught — an unusual scoring idea.
- **«Мяцёлка»** — on ice, twig besoms and a lump of ice; everyone sweeps *at
  once* to drive it into their own hole. Folk curling, simultaneous rather than
  turn-based.
- **«Да Вільні едуць»** — a circle with a sharpened stake at the centre called
  **«Вільня»**; each boy drives his peg into the rim and advances it by however
  deep it sank. A game whose goal is getting to Vilnia.
- **«У казу»** — caps are the "goats"; a herdsman **tethered by a rein** must
  stop them being snatched. The herdsman is chosen by throwing a cap backwards
  between spread legs.
- **«Круцёлка»** — herdsmen bore a felled pine through the middle and pin it to
  the stump so it revolves; boys ride the ends. Recorded as *«самая любімая
  гульня пастухоў на стойле»*. ⚠️ No win condition is recorded.

### Craft-anchored, which is where this section's identity is

- **«Палатно»** — the hand-linked line **is** a bolt of cloth: it winds into a
  spiral round the «матка» while a **«кравец»** steals children off the loose
  end, and her count comes up short, the shortfall explained away as *«па ніткі
  пайшло»* — it went for thread. Real weaving-economy semantics.
- **«Вузельчык»** — two players stretch belts into **a needle's eye**; the line
  threads through singing a genuine needle-riddle (thin and long, one-eared,
  steel nose and a **flaxen tail**); the last child ties the knot.
- **«Каноплі», the Ramanaŭ form** — a **«дзедзька»** carrying a **чапяла**, the
  iron pot-hook from the hearth, gathers a plaited chain of children behind him,
  then **coughs** and drives them off one by one with the hook. The cough is the
  state change. The modern kindergarten «Каноплі» is a far weaker thing — the
  «Проса» call-and-response with the crop swapped.
- **«Лянок», the Horki version** — the one to use. See below.

## «Лянок» — the Horki version

Three different games share this name. The Horki one is the richest by a wide
margin, **and it is the only place the «Церніца» and «Кросны» material exists** —
neither survives as a standalone game.

A **гаспадыня** opens by chanting that she sowed flax in a hollow and that it
came up blue as a little bell. She then interrogates the ring through the **whole
real flax chain**, and the ring answers and mimes each stage:

**браць** (pulling — flax is pulled, never cut) → **малаціць** (threshing out
seed) → **рассцілаць** (spreading in the field to ret — *field*-retting, the
Belarusian practice, not water-retting) → **мяць** (braking, the **церніца**
operation) → **ткаць** (weaving, the **кросны**) → **бяліць** (bleaching) →
**кроіць** (cutting) → **шыць** (sewing).

Eight discrete stages, each with a verb, a gesture and a line of text. That is a
web game already written.

**Honest specificity:** the *type* is pan-East-Slavic and Lozka says so. What is
Belarusian is the **lexicon** — кужалёк, пасмачка воўны, the бабуля frame — and
the **completeness of the chain**, which the kindergarten versions truncate.

## Tier 3 — do not claim these

Pan-Slavic mechanics with a Belarusian text. Usable as *Belarusian versions*,
never as unique: **А мы проса сеялі** (the weakest possible pick — Russian
Wikipedia opens by naming it common to Russians, Belarusians and Ukrainians),
**А мы сеялі лянок** (the carahod, as opposed to the Horki game), **Мак**
(Ukrainian «Ой на горі мак» is canonical), **Грушка**, **Рэдзька**, **Гарлачыкі**,
**Агароднік**, **Каваль**, **Купец**, **Клубок**, **Явар**, **Каменьчыкі**,
**Бабкі**, **Бірулькі**, **Цурка/Чыжык**, **Золата**, **Кот і мыш**, **Шуляк**,
**Гусі**, **Жмуркі**, **Ліскі**, **Мост**, **Прэла-гарэла**.

**Imported or Soviet-era, and must be labelled as such if used at all:**
**Штандар, стой!** (German *Stand hier!* via Russian «Штандер»), **У пяць
дзевак** («Я знаю пять имён»), **Гарадкі** and **Лапта** (Soviet mass sports by
1923 and 1957), **Пасадка бульбы** (a PE relay in folk dress), **Міхасік**
(a modern pedagogical adaptation), **Дзікуны і рускія**, **Піянеры**.

## Does not exist — do not write rules for these

Searched in Belarusian and Russian across the 1991 book, BNT 1972, the 85-game
Lida index, the Horki set, six schools.by card indexes, two PDFs and both
Wikipedias.

- **Beekeeping / бортніцтва.** No game. Bee *lore* is rich and tree beekeeping is
  UNESCO-inscribed (2020), but no folk game attaches to it.
- **Potato.** No traditional game. The potato is an 18th–19th c. arrival, too
  late for the ritual game stratum.
- **«Церніца», «Кросны», «Ткачыха», «Жорны», «Мельнік», «Дзяжа».** No games under
  these names. Weaving survives only *inside* «Лянок», «Палатно», «Вузельчык».
  Дзяжа is well attested as **rite** — the bride sits on it — not as гульня.
- **«Хвартух», «Пячора», «Бусел», «Хроміка», «Дуда», «Жораў», «Скакалка».**
  Nothing, in any spelling. «Бусел» is central to Belarusian symbolism and turns
  up in counting-rhymes, but is not a named game.
- **«Клёк».** The word is real in Homieĺ-region dialect, but **no Belarusian
  rules text exists**; what circulates online under the name is Russian «Пекарь»
  or a Mordovian game. Most likely a local name for «Пікер».
- **«Дучкі».** ⚠️ The *word* is attested — **дучка** is a marble and the pit it
  is driven into (Kaspiarovič, *Віцебскі краёвы слоўнік*, 1927) — and the same
  word names the eye-hole in the upper stone of the **жорны**. The **rules are
  not recorded.** Do not invent them.

### Naming traps

- **«Млын»** in Belarusian game collections is an **active circle game** — a
  windmill, players as sails — **not** nine men's morris. Searching Belarusian
  sources for «млын» returns the wrong thing.
- **«Воўк і авечкі»** in Belarusian collections is a **running chase game**, not
  the fox-and-geese board game that shares the name in Russian.
- **«Гуські»** is a winter ice stick-and-peg game, **not** fox-and-geese.
- **«У шкера»** is an OCR corruption of **«У пікера»**.
- **«Кулькі»** in the Horki collection is a two-man beam-pushing contest, not a
  ball game.

## Board games: there is no Belarusian one

Stated plainly because the answer is clean. **No verifiable distinctly
Belarusian traditional board-game tradition exists.** The corpus is
overwhelmingly outdoor. Lozka's classification has **no board-game category at
all** — his "with inventory" branch is organised by *what the stick does*.

- **Nine men's morris: not a single find anywhere in Belarus.** The standard
  listing runs Gokstad, Staraya Ladoga, Novgorod, Pskov, Staraya Ryazan. The
  Polack Transfiguration church graffiti corpus — some 4,000 inscriptions — has
  **no game fields**.
- **Draughts: no Belarusian variant.** Belarus plays Russian and international
  draughts; the 100-square game arrived in 1952. «Чапаеў» is an all-Soviet game.
- ⚠️ **«Беларускія шахматы»** — a 9×9 board with ranks named after historical
  Belarusian military titles — is a **modern invention: 2010, commercialised
  2016.** Not a reconstruction. It must never be presented as traditional.
- **Tafl and dice on Belarusian territory: not verified.**

### But there is a real artefact story

Chess is documented from **15+ sites now in Belarus** — Бярэсце, Ваўкавыск,
Віцебск, Гродна, Друцк, Копысь, Лукомль, Менск, Мсціслаў, Навагрудак, Полацк,
Рагачоў, Слонім, Тураў — carved from bone, antler and wood, 11th–14th c. And the
carving is **regional**: a **Vitsebsk rook shaped as a trident**, a **Hrodna rook
shaped as a warship with oars**, **Vaŭkavysk pawns as drummers**, a **Lukoml
queen as a commander**.

On **10 September 2018**, excavations at Hrodna Old Castle turned up a
**fragment of a wooden game board** — a strip over 25 cm long with **eight light
and dark inlaid fields**, from a house of the second half of the 14th century.

That is the honest tabletop anchor: not a Belarusian game, but a Belarusian
*hand* on a shared game.

⚠️ Separately, a **plinfa** from Hrodna Old Castle (2019, 12th – mid-14th c.)
carries a scratched bordered grid of ~14 cells per row. Museum staff offer two
readings — a game board, or a tally board for counting brick types. **Purpose
undetermined.** Do not present it as a confirmed board.

## Heritage listings

**UNESCO — seven Belarusian elements, two are rites, none is a game proper:**
Калядныя цары (Urgent Safeguarding, 2009), Juraŭski Karahod (Urgent
Safeguarding, 2019), Budslaŭ fest (2018), tree beekeeping with Poland (2020),
straw weaving (2022), vytsinanka (2024), Nieglubka textiles (2025).

⚠️ **Жаніцьба Цярэшкі is NOT on the UNESCO list.** Belarusian press repeatedly
describes it as *aspiring* to inscription. Any claim that it is already listed is
false and would be easy for a reader to check.

**State List of Historical and Cultural Values — the game entries:** Яшчар
(в. Гута, Пастаўскі р-н), Жаніцьба Цярэшкі (**23БК000061**, Лепельскі р-н), Ката
пячы (**33АК000117**, Скірмантава), Пахаванне Дзеда (Гарадоцкі р-н), Стрылка
(Бездзеж), Цягнуць Каляду на дуба (Новіны), Куры (Клічаўскі р-н — ⚠️ rules not
retrievable anywhere), Калядныя цары (**63БК000053**), Юраўскі карагод
(**33БК000002**), Ваджэнне стралы (**33БК000003**).

⚠️ The published Belarusian Wikipedia table of intangible values is **incomplete
and out of date** — it omits both Яшчар and Ката пячы, which had to be confirmed
separately through livingheritage.by and heritage.gov.by. Two of its codes carry
transcription errors. Treat it as a partial index.

⚠️ **«Пахаванне Дзеда» is explicitly bawdy** — the straw doll carries a carrot as
a phallus. Relevant if the section is family-facing.

⚠️ The popular claim that «Калядныя цары» originates with an 18th-c. Russian
Imperial garrison **comes from a single local history teacher and is doubted by
researchers.** The stronger scholarly reading is a transformation of **батлейка**
puppet theatre into village folk drama, evidenced by kinship with «Цар
Максіміліан».

## Prior art

Essentially none. Belarusian folk games live in kindergarten methodology pages,
regional library card indexes and festival programmes — **not in digital form.**
A search for web adaptations returns school and nursery pages describing how to
play them in a hall. The niche is genuinely empty, which is the good news: this
section would be the first of its kind rather than a re-skin of something.
