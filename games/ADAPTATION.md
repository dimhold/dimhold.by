# Which of them survive contact with a browser

Design notes for the Гульні section. Read `RESEARCH.md` first — it establishes
what may and may not be claimed about each game.

---

## What the site can actually run

The site is static Astro on GitHub Pages. There is no backend and there will not
be one. That rules out live multiplayer, which is inconvenient, because **almost
every game in the corpus is a group game**: a circle of a dozen people, a
matchmaker, two facing ranks.

So each adaptation has to answer one question honestly: **what is left when you
take the circle away?** Three answers work.

1. **You play the one interesting role, the crowd is the machine.** You are the
   свацця, or the Яшчар, or the гаспадыня; the ring is simulated. Best for
   games whose structure is a dialogue.
2. **The game was already about a skill, not a crowd.** Timing, aim, sweeping,
   shielding a flame. Ports directly to one player.
3. **The game was already a process.** The flax chain is eight ordered stages —
   that is a sequence game whether or not anyone is holding hands.

What must not happen: a game reduced to a quiz about a game. If the adaptation
is "read this text, then answer which comes after retting", the tradition has
been documented, not played.

## What the site already gives us for free

- `src/lib/ornament.js` — a real cross-stitch chart generator: `chart()`,
  `tileSvg()`, `bandSvg()`, and a day seed. **A game can weave.**
- `src/lib/words/` — 372 Belarusian words with glosses in three languages, split
  by month.
- The palette tokens (`--tint-h`, `--tint-s`, `--ink`, `--surface`), the mask
  ornament, the rushnyk band, the hand font for headings.
- Three languages already wired, with the Belarusian text as first-class.

A game built on these will look like it belongs to the site rather than like an
embedded widget.

## The shortlist

Ordered by what I would build, not by importance.

### 1. «Ката пячы» — first build

**Tier 1, state-listed, and nobody has ever adapted it.**

The rite: a cat modelled from buns hangs from the ceiling; you ride up on a
pitchfork and jump to bite a piece while others obstruct you; whoever bites the
tail wins. The calendar logic is good too — 27 November, the food deliberately
fatty because Piĺipaŭka, the fast, starts the next morning with a ban on both
meat *and* games.

**Web:** a timing-and-aim game. The cat swings on its cord; you time a jump;
obstruction shifts your aim. Rounds get harder as the easy pieces go and only
the tail is left. Ends with the tail bite.

Why first: it is genuinely unique, it is mechanically the simplest thing here to
make *good*, it is funny, and it is unmistakably not a re-skin of a game the
visitor already knows.

### 2. «Лянок» (the Horki version) — the one that ties into the site

Eight stages, each with a verb, a gesture and a line of text: браць → малаціць →
рассцілаць → мяць → ткаць → бяліць → кроіць → шыць.

**Web:** a call-and-response rhythm game. The гаспадыня asks; you answer on the
beat with the right action. Get the chain right and — this is the point —
**the finished cloth is an ornament, generated by `ornament.js`, that the player
keeps.** The flax you processed becomes the pattern the page is already woven
from.

⚠️ Framing: this must be presented as *the Belarusian version, and the most
complete one*, not as unique. Lozka says the flax carahods were modelled on
«Проса». Say so on the page; it costs nothing and it is the difference between a
site that did the reading and one that did not.

### 3. «Панначка» — the visual showpiece

Girls circle, each carrying a **lit candle**, each trying to blow out a
neighbour's flame while shielding her own. The last flame relights all the rest.

**Web:** a dark screen, a ring of flames, one of them yours. Shield or blow —
you cannot do both at once, which is the whole game. The ending is not
elimination but **restoration**: the last flame goes round and relights the
circle. Almost nothing else in the corpus ends like that.

No analogue found anywhere. Mechanically tiny, visually the strongest thing we
could ship.

### 4. «Грахі» — the one with the idea in it

Roll a ball along a row of holes. Miss and a **грэх** goes into your hole; hit
and it goes into your victim's. Three sins and you are shot against the wall.
And: **players carrying sins get fewer throws.**

**Web:** turn-based against simulated opponents, trivial to implement. The hook
is the rule itself — a folk game where guilt reduces your standing to punish
others is worth putting in front of people. The page can say that plainly.

⚠️ Hole count and the exact throw allocation are **not recorded**; we will have
to choose values and say on the page that we did.

### 5. «Жаніцьба Цярэшкі» — the flagship, built last

The only game an authority calls unique to Belarus, and it models cleanly
because **the songs are the state transitions**:

```
S0 setup → S1 free dancing → S2 two ranks form
S3 pairing loop:
     свацця's solo couplet          = the proposal
     the girls' answering couplet   = the response
     ├─ accepted → blessing from бацька/маці → chorus verse over the dancing
     │             pair = ratification → the pair leaves the pool
     └─ refused  → she twists away, the girls screen her, he fails to catch her
                   → the mocking verse = the failure state → he returns empty
S4 chasing → S5 feast → S6 dispersal after midnight
```

**Web:** you are the свацця. You pair the room off; each proposal succeeds or
fails; the mocking verse is your fail sound. The real entry condition is worth
keeping — it ran in the карчма, at the end of the evening, after long dancing,
because no respectable house would host it.

Built last because it needs music to work at all: 3/4 or 9/8, fiddle, дуда,
цымбалы. Without the tunes it is a spreadsheet.

### 6. «Яшчар» — beautiful, and the easiest to get wrong

The two-part song is the design: part one circles in praise; part two shifts to a
melody close to **wedding laments** as each girl redeems her wreath. That
opposition is the game.

Worth doing, but only with real audio and with the framing from `RESEARCH.md`
attached — *the most archaic surviving form of a shared East Slavic game, and the
only one under state protection*.

### Also viable, lower priority

**«Мяцёлка»** (simultaneous sweeping — needs physics), **«Гуські»** (same),
**«Браднік»** (the quarry steers the hunter with «Глыбока!» / «Мелка!» — lovely,
needs audio), **«Азярод»** (a vault-the-hayrack skill shot), **«Палатно»** and
**«Вузельчык»** (short craft pieces that would work as a pair with «Лянок»).

### Not a game, but the honest tabletop answer

There is no Belarusian board game. What there is: **regionally carved chess
pieces** — the Hrodna rook as a warship with oars, the Vitsebsk rook as a
trident, the Vaŭkavysk pawns as drummers, the Lukoml queen as a commander — and
a **fragment of a wooden board with eight inlaid fields**, second half of the
14th century, dug up in Hrodna Old Castle in 2018.

If the section wants something to sit still and be looked at, that is it: not a
Belarusian game, but a Belarusian hand on a shared one. It would make a good
closing exhibit and it is true.

## Open questions for Dmitriy

1. **Framing.** The section cannot be "games only Belarus has" — see
   `RESEARCH.md`. Proposed instead: **"як гулялі ў Беларусі"** — how Belarus
   played. Every game carries its source and an honest line about how shared it
   is. Agreed?
2. **How many.** Four small games done properly beats ten stubs. My vote: ship
   **Ката пячы** alone first, then **Лянок**, then **Панначка**.
3. **Audio.** Цярэшка and Яшчар do not work silently. Live recordings exist
   (the Hlybokaje «Цярэшка» ensemble, the Huta folklore circle) but licensing is
   unknown. Commissioning a fiddle-and-дуда loop is the alternative.
4. **Language of the games themselves.** The site is trilingual, but these games
   *are* their Belarusian words. Proposal: the game text stays Belarusian
   always; the explanation around it follows the page language.
5. **Where they live.** `/games/` as a fourth nav pill beside «мае праекты» and
   «блог», one page per game.
